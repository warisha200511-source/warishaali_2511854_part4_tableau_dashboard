Chart Selection Justification

1. Sales Trend Over Time – Line Chart

Why this chart was chosen:
A line chart is the most suitable chart for showing how sales change over time. It makes it easy to see month-to-month movement, identify peaks and drops, and understand whether the business is growing steadily or showing fluctuations.

Why it fits the business question:
Leadership wants to monitor sales performance over time. A line chart clearly answers questions such as:

- Are sales increasing or decreasing?
- Which months performed best or worst?
- Is performance stable or seasonal?

This makes it a strong choice for identifying revenue trends and time-based business patterns.

---

2. Category / Sub-Category Profitability – Bar Chart

Why this chart was chosen:
A bar chart is effective for comparing profit across different categories and sub-categories because it allows quick comparison of values side by side. Differences in bar height make high-performing and low-performing categories easy to spot.

Why it fits the business question:
The business needs to know which product areas are generating the most profit and which are underperforming. A bar chart helps answer:

- Which categories contribute the most profit?
- Which sub-categories are weak?
- Where should the business focus or reduce attention?

This chart supports product portfolio decisions and profitability analysis.

---

3. Regional Sales Performance – Filled Map

Why this chart was chosen:
A map is useful when the business wants to understand geographic distribution of sales. It visually shows how performance varies across locations and helps identify strong and weak regions in a way that is easy to understand.

Why it fits the business question:
The leadership team wants to monitor regional performance. A map helps answer:

- Which regions are generating the highest sales?
- Is the business concentrated in only a few markets or spread across many areas?
- Which locations may need more attention or support?

This chart is appropriate because location is an important part of the business story.

---

4. Return Rate by Customer Segment – Bar Chart

Why this chart was chosen:
A bar chart is a clear way to compare return rates across customer segments such as Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office. It makes it easy to see which segment has the highest or lowest return rate.

Why it fits the business question:
The business wants to understand customer segment behavior and return patterns. This chart helps answer:

- Which customer segment returns the most?
- Are some segments riskier than others?
- Where should the business investigate customer issues more deeply?

This supports customer strategy and return management decisions.

---

5. Return Rate by Region – Horizontal Bar Chart

Why this chart was chosen:
A horizontal bar chart works well when comparing a small number of categories such as regions. It provides a clean ranking view and makes it easy to compare return rates side by side.

Why it fits the business question:
Leadership needs to know whether return behavior differs across regions. This chart helps answer:

- Which region has the highest return rate?
- Which regions are performing better in post-sale quality?
- Where is the business exposed to higher return risk?

This chart supports regional risk monitoring and operational decision-making.

---

6. Discount vs. Profit – Scatter Plot

Why this chart was chosen:
A scatter plot is useful for studying the relationship between two numerical measures — in this case, average discount and profit. It helps show whether higher discounts are linked to higher or lower profit across categories.

Why it fits the business question:
The business wants to understand the impact of discounting on profitability. This chart helps answer:

- Are higher discounts improving profit or reducing it?
- Which categories remain profitable despite discounts?
- Are some categories being discounted too heavily for the profit they generate?

This chart supports pricing and promotional decision-making.

---

7. Shipping Mode and Delivery Delay Impact – Highlight Table / Text Table

Why this chart was chosen:
A table is suitable here because the business needs to compare shipping performance across ship modes and months at the same time. It allows the dashboard to show detailed operational values in a structured way without losing the monthly breakdown.

Why it fits the business question:
The business wants to monitor shipping and delivery performance. This chart helps answer:

- Which shipping mode is used most heavily?
- In which months are delivery values higher or lower?
- Is shipping performance consistent across the year?

This chart supports logistics monitoring and helps identify operational concentration or service issues.

---

8. Dashboard-Level Combination of Charts – Why this overall chart mix works

The dashboard is not built as a random collection of charts. Each chart answers a different business question, and together they create a connected leadership view:

- Line chart explains overall sales movement over time
- Bar chart for category profit explains where money is being made
- Map explains where sales are coming from geographically
- Segment and region return charts explain where post-sale risks exist
- Scatter plot explains the relationship between discounts and profitability
- Shipping table explains operational delivery performance

Together, these charts help leadership move from “How much are we selling?” to “Where are we making profit, where are we losing value through returns, and what business actions should be taken next?”

---

Conclusion

The selected charts were chosen to match the type of business question being answered. Time-based trends are shown through a line chart, comparisons across categories and segments are shown through bar charts, geographic performance is shown through a map, relationships between measures are shown through a scatter plot, and operational shipping details are shown through a structured table. This combination makes the dashboard easier to understand, more decision-focused, and more useful for a leadership audience.
